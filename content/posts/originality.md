Lately I've been thinking about what it really means to think originally. 

I'm by no means the first person to do many of the things I explore. A lot of the questions I care about were stirred up by reading other people's work, learning from researchers, engineers, and writers whose thinking inspired me. Inspiration is part of the process. None of us work in isolation. What matters, at least to me, is what happens after that moment of inspiration. Once an idea sparks my curiosity, I try to follow it in my own way - asking my own questions, exploring paths and their tangents that genuinely interest me, and letting my thinking evolve naturally from there. That's where a sense of originality comes from: not from inventing ideas out of thin air, but from having a strong enough sense of self to shape them into something uniquely your own. 

In other words, all the components can be, and sometimes should be, borrowed. But it's the *chain* of curiosity, intuition, and thoughts, that leads someone down a particular path that's much harder to replicate.

The truth is, trying to replicate someone's path is a losing game anyway. If you're constantly watching someone else and moving in response to them, you'll always be one step behind, not only behind them, but also behind the full potential version of yourself. There's something so much more freeing about letting go of that comparison and constant keeping up and simply choosing your own direction and owning your path, with all its non-linearities and sub-optimals. Because once you stop trying to follow someone else's path, you realize you were never in a race to begin with.

The world has always benefitted from original thinking. And especially now in the AI age, where things can be replicated at a speed where it's getting exponentially harder to verify, review and evaluate, I take pride and comfort in knowing that I still very much care about originality and will inhesitantly choose the long-term game >> short-term game if it means my originality can be protected and nurtured. 

Lastly, I have a message to all the original thinkers who selflessly choose to work in the open, exposing your ideas to the world, even at the risk of them being replicated without your knowledge. Let's keep going, and let's ignore the uncomfortable feeling that maybe we're being taken advantage of or not given credit for. In the long run, we gain far more from sharing. One thing that can never be taken away from us is this: thinking deeply and originally means we will always be able to find new paths to explore. 

It's good to end this train of thoughts on a positive note, move on and focus on living my own moral values.
